## Unvalidated hypotheses

1. Semantic criticality reduces number, currency, date and negation errors at the same real-time factor.
2. Mora Shadow lowers kana-CER and Mora Error Rate without erasing learner errors.
3. Information-gain scheduling has lower RTF than always running two ASR models.
4. Grammar Honeytrap improves learner-error preservation without materially worsening ordinary CER.
5. Held-out calibration lowers ECE and AURC under microphone and domain shift.
6. Qwen second-ear agreement improves critical-entity recognition.
7. Preservation heads distinguish fillers, repairs and learner errors from noise.
8. Rights-gated lexical features improve proper nouns without storing reconstructable source text.

None of these is presented as a measured improvement until a held-out corpus is executed.

## Evidence standard

A passing unit test proves a contract or deterministic algorithm, not recognition accuracy. A quality claim requires:

- exact model revisions and runtime versions
- immutable audio/evaluation manifests
- speaker-disjoint train/calibration/test splits
- paired bootstrap confidence intervals
- CER, kana-CER, MLER and critical semantic metrics
- ECE/Brier/NLL/AURC and risk at operational coverage
- RTF, peak VRAM and cache hit rate
- publication of negative results and failure examples where rights permit
